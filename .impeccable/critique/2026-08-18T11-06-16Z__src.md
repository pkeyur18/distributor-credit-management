## Overall Impression

A genuinely well-executed, disciplined Operate-mode build — the design system's named rules aren't just documented, they're implemented faithfully in code. The gap isn't specificity or visual craft, it's a handful of real friction points on the single most-repeated action in the product, plus one broken token and one weak confirmation moment on the highest-stakes recovery path.

## What's Working

- **Literal fidelity to DESIGN.md's named rules** — Blended Alert Border, Nested-Radius, Half-Height-Bar, and commit-weight-not-color all show up as real code, not approximations.
- **Disciplined component reuse** — RestoreOptionList, StructureTreeNode, and BarListChart are genuinely shared across screens, which is exactly what keeps a single-operator tool learnable over months of infrequent use.
- **The month-close wizard** — appropriately weighted, well-sequenced, honest about consequences at every step; the strongest emotional beat in the app.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Settings nav has 8 ungrouped sibling items** — `settings.tsx:953-962`. Why it matters: violates the working-memory guideline on an occasional-use screen, for a low-technical-skill sole operator with no documentation to fall back on (PRODUCT.md). Fix: group into 2-3 labeled sections (Calculation Rules / Reporting / System). Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`.

**[P1] No sequential/batch entry path for the system's declared most-frequent action** — `business-volume-entry.tsx`. Product Principle 5 demands "one field, one action, no ceremony," but after Save, recording the *next* member requires clicking "Change" then re-searching from scratch. For a session recording dozens of entries, this repeats avoidable friction on every single one. Fix: add a "Record another" action that returns focus straight to search. Suggested command: `/impeccable optimize`.

**[P2] Restore success feedback is a transient toast racing a redirect** — `settings.tsx:862-884`. `confirmRestore` fires a 3.4s toast and immediately calls `markSignedOut()`, redirecting to Login — the highest-stakes recovery moment ends with weak, possibly-missed confirmation. Fix: carry a persistent one-time notice onto the Login screen instead of relying on the toast. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`.

**[P2] Broken CSS variable on the one permanently-visible banner** — `outstanding-month-banner.tsx:38` sets `color: var(--text)`, a token that doesn't exist in `index.css` (only `--ink`/`--muted-text` do). Fix: replace with `var(--ink)`. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`.

**[P3] "TBV" abbreviation breaks vocabulary consistency** — `business-volume-entry.tsx:240` — every other screen spells out "Total Business Volume." Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User)**: No keyboard shortcut reaches the Volume Entry screen (mouse-only sidebar nav); each successive entry costs an extra "Change" click since there's no "next" flow. Closing a month, by contrast, is genuinely efficient (3 real clicks) — no complaint there.

**Sam (Accessibility)**: Base-UI primitives give solid focus rings and dialog semantics throughout; no critical failures found. The one concrete issue is the broken `--text` token on the outstanding-month banner — an undefined CSS variable on the single always-visible status element risks unpredictable contrast for low-vision users.

**Riley (Stress Tester)**: In `monthly-close.tsx`'s `chooseBackupLocation()` (lines 114-128), cancelling the native save dialog still sets `backupConfirmed = true` in the `finally` block — intentional per Rule-31 (cancel never blocks close), but the UI's "Backup copy generated and confirmed" line reads identically whether or not an external copy actually exists, which Riley would flag as misleading confidence at the exact moment stakes are highest.

## Minor Observations

- Reports screen leaks an internal requirement ID into user-facing copy: `reports.tsx:233` — "(NFR-8)" appears in a caption the operator sees.
- Pagination (page-size selector + Prev/Next) is duplicated near-identically across `audit.tsx`, `correction-panel.tsx`, and `business-volume-entry.tsx` with no shared component.
- Save-button labels on Settings are inconsistently specific ("Save slab table" vs. bare "Save" on Session/Royalty cards).
- Cognitive load: two checklist failures — Settings' 8 flat nav items (see P1), and Home's near-equal visual weight across stat row/search/two charts, softening hierarchy on the daily landing screen.
- Emotional journey: restore is the one valley in an otherwise well-paced app (see P2 above); month-close is the peak.

## Questions to Consider

- Business Volume Entry is the declared single most frequent action — was the current search-select-save loop ever tested against a real session of dozens of back-to-back entries, or only against entering one figure?
- Would grouping Settings' eight flat nav items into three named categories change whether an operator who visits "occasionally" and won't read documentation can find the royalty rate six months from now?
- Every irreversible action gets a dedicated wizard except confirming that a restore actually worked — does that asymmetry match how scary the two actions actually are?
